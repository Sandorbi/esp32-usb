The LED circuit and pin connections were straightforward to put together.
PWM was also straightforward, including mapping intensity levels 1–5 to brightness.
The hardest part was working on minimal command latency over the USB cable.
That mattered most for STOP, which needs to interrupt the sequence promptly.
The firmware uses non-blocking timing so it can keep checking commands and safety.
The test uses a simulated sensor value rather than a physical temperature sensor.
For the full system, I have not worked with heating elements before and would need to learn more.
I would need to research heater drivers, power requirements, temperature sensing, and calibration.
I have limited experience building apps that connect to an ESP32 over USB, so I would research connection reliability and latency further.
